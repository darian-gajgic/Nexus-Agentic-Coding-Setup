# Brand Rubric — quality gate

> Use: run the gates first — any FAIL means do not deliver; fix and re-run. Then score the dimensions 0–4.
> Pass bar: all gates pass AND total ≥ 21/28 AND no dimension ≤ 1.
> Every check is text-observable. For each verdict, quote the line that passes or fails. No taste required.

## Must-pass gates — binary, do not deliver if any fails

- **G1 — Positioning complete.** The statement contains ALL template fields: audience, situation/need, category, benefit, reason to believe, named alternative, difference. Any field missing = FAIL.
- **G2 — Swap test.** Replace the brand name with the nearest competitor's (or the named alternative). If the statement remains credibly true, FAIL.
- **G3 — Exclusion named.** The deliverable states in writing who this brand is NOT for. Absent = FAIL.
- **G4 — Personality bounded.** Exactly 3 traits; each has a "but not" boundary AND one concrete behavior. More traits, or any trait missing either part = FAIL.
- **G5 — Voice is demonstrated.** Every voice rule has ≥ 1 DO and ≥ 1 DON'T example sentence. Any adjectives-only rule = FAIL.
- **G6 — One falsifiable promise.** Exactly one promise, containing a number, timeframe, or observable behavior a customer could catch being broken. Zero promises, 2+, or unfalsifiable phrasing = FAIL.
- **G7 — Naming screens documented** (naming work only). Domain, handle, and trademark screen results recorded with a check date, marked "(verify current availability)". Undocumented = FAIL.
- **G8 — Visual direction grounded.** Direction includes ≥ 3 references (linked or described) AND a "never" list. Either missing = FAIL.
- **G9 — Kill list clean.** No kill-list phrase (below) appears in customer-facing copy. Any hit = FAIL.
- **G10 — Audit findings complete** (audit work only). Every finding has severity (S1/S2/S3) + effort + a concrete fix + captured evidence (screenshot or quote). Any finding missing one = FAIL.

## Scored dimensions (0–4 each; quote one line of evidence per score)

**D1 — Audience specificity**
- 2 = a segment by role or demographics ("freelance designers," "Gen-Z shoppers").
- 4 = a person in a situation with the current alternative named ("freelance designers who invoice from Google Docs and chase payments by email").

**D2 — Differentiation sharpness**
- 2 = benefit is a category table-stake ("easy," "fast," "quality"); the opposite test fails.
- 4 = a claim the named alternative cannot copy without visible cost, and the opposite of the claim is a real strategy someone else actually runs.

**D3 — Reason to believe**
- 2 = claims asserted with no mechanism ("we care more," "obsessed with detail").
- 4 = every claim is paired with a proof: a feature, process, number, origin, guarantee, or receipt.

**D4 — Voice operationality**
- 2 = rules need interpretation ("be warm but professional").
- 4 = rules are mechanical (countable or greppable: word bans, sentence caps, punctuation limits); two different writers applying them produce copy a reader attributes to the same brand.

**D5 — Coherence**
- 2 = at least one element points elsewhere (playful voice + austere visuals + corporate promise) with no stated reason.
- 4 = every voice and visual rule traces to a personality trait; every trait traces to the positioning; the promise operationalizes the benefit. The trace can be shown line by line.

**D6 — Actionability**
- 2 = usable only after a meeting ("directions to explore," open questions in the deliverable).
- 4 = a stranger could write tomorrow's post, brief a designer, or answer a support email from this document alone, today.

**D7 — Distinctive-asset potential**
- 2 = assets are category defaults (the category's color, a generic sans, stock imagery styles).
- 4 = at least one ownable, repeatable combination is specified (e.g. color + device + phrase) with an explicit "repeat this everywhere" instruction.

## Kill list — amateur markers

Any of these in customer-facing copy fails gate G9. In strategy documents, cap D2 at 1.

Phrases (grep for these):
- "passionate about" (quality / excellence / craft / what we do)
- "for all your ___ needs"
- "solutions" as the noun for what you sell
- "innovative," "disruptive," "cutting-edge" as self-description
- "premium" with no price or behavior consequence attached
- "quality you can trust"
- "young, fun and fresh"
- "elevate," "unleash," "empower," "supercharge" in a tagline
- "we're not like other ___" (show it; never claim it)
- "our story began with a passion for…"
- "best-in-class," "world-class" without a ranking source

Patterns (structural checks):
- Mood board delivered as the strategy — visuals with no positioning attached
- Adjective-triad personality with no boundaries ("bold, authentic, human")
- Voice described as "conversational" with zero rules
- "It's like X for Y" used as the public positioning (internal shorthand only)
- Two or more promises
- A tagline that could sit unchanged on any competitor's site
- Serving-everyone hedges ("whether you're a startup or a Fortune 500…")
- Logo tweaks presented as a rebrand
- Copy describing the company's feelings instead of the customer's outcome
- Exclamation marks in error, support, or money copy

## Verdict

1. Any gate FAIL → do not deliver. Fix, re-run all gates.
2. All gates pass, total ≥ 21/28, no dimension ≤ 1 → ship — unless a PLAYBOOK escalation trigger applies, then send through `cjudge` first.
3. All gates pass but the score falls short → revise the two lowest dimensions, re-score once; still short → escalate to `cjudge` with both versions attached.
