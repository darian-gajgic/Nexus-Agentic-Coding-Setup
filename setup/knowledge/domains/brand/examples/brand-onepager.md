## Audience insight

Primary audience: working club and wedding DJs playing 2–6 paid gigs a month, booked through Instagram DMs and WhatsApp.

They currently manage gigs in DMs plus a notes app, which costs them real money — double-booked dates, "forgotten" fees, no deposits — and makes them feel like amateurs in front of clients they want to impress.

The sharpest moment: quoting a price in a DM thread and watching the client go quiet. No contract, no deposit, no leverage.

**NOT for:** touring artists with managers/agents, or hobbyists playing free gigs. They may use it; we never aim copy at them.

## Positioning

For working DJs who book gigs through DMs and lose money to no-shows and awkward money talk, GigDock is the booking tool that turns a DM inquiry into a signed, deposit-paid booking in under five minutes — because a booking link with contract and deposit built in does the negotiating for you. Unlike DMs plus a notes app (or a generic invoice tool), GigDock speaks gig: dates, set times, riders, and deposits — not line items.

- Opposite test: the opposite — "informal, contract-free, deposit-free booking" — is literally the status quo we replace. The claim has teeth.
- Swap test: PayPal or Wave can send an invoice; they cannot hold a date, attach a rider, or auto-release a Saturday slot when the deposit doesn't land.

## Reasons to believe

1. Every booking link carries a contract template and a deposit requirement by default — leverage is the product, not a setting.
2. Unpaid deposit by the due date → the date auto-releases and the DJ is notified. The mechanism enforces the promise.
3. Built by people who have chased wedding deposits over WhatsApp; the rider field is not an afterthought.

## Personality

1. **Roadie-practical, but not basic** — we solve gig-night reality (deposits, set times, power requirements); we never dumb down money talk.
2. **Straight about money, but not mercenary** — we name prices and deposits without apology; we never gloat about squeezing clients.
3. **Backstage-calm, but not aloof** — unshakeable in failures and disputes; we never perform hype ("LET'S GOOO"), even in marketing.

## Promise

**Every booking confirmed through GigDock has a signed agreement and a paid deposit — or the date auto-releases.**

A breach is catchable: one confirmed date without a deposit on file means we broke it. That's the point.

## Voice — do / don't

| Rule (mechanical, checkable) | DO | DON'T |
|---|---|---|
| Gig words, not software words: "booking, date, set" — never "workflow, solution, platform" | "Your Saturday is booked and the deposit's in." | "Your booking workflow has been optimized." |
| Money in numbers, no euphemisms | "€300 deposit, due Friday." | "A small upfront investment to secure our collaboration." |
| ≤ 16 words per sentence in product and money copy | "The client signed. The date is locked." | "Once your client has completed the signing process, the date will be…" |
| Second person, contractions always | "You're covered if they cancel." | "Users are protected in the event of cancellation." |
| Zero exclamation marks in product, money, and error copy | "Deposit received." | "Deposit received!!" |
| Humor only in marketing, only about gig life, never about the client | "Play the set. Skip the 'can you send an invoice?' text." | "Clients, am I right? 🙄" |

Words we use: gig, date, set, deposit, booked, locked, rider, floor, club, drop (marketing only).
Words we never use: solution, workflow, empower, hustle, grind, revolutionize, seamless, "passionate about music," vibes (in product copy), journey.

## Tone flex (voice constant, tone flexes — same person, different room)

| Context | Dials | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing / social | humor ON (gig-life only); rhythm looser; still no hype-shouting | "Three DMs. One link. Deposit in before soundcheck." |
| Product UI / pricing | short declaratives; numbers first; jokes OFF | "€19/mo. Unlimited bookings. Deposits included." |
| Support / errors | fewest words; the fix first; jokes OFF; no blame | "Payment failed. We've sent your client a retry link." |
| Money / legal | plain formal; full sentences; contractions optional; humor forbidden | "The deposit is refundable until 14 days before the event date." |

## Visual direction

Tensions (max 3): nightlife-native but not neon-cheesy · money-serious but not bank-boring · tough but not aggressive.

- **Color:** near-black base (off-black, never #000 flat), everything else grayscale, ONE hot accent — signal orange — reserved for money and confirmation moments only. Deliberate defection from BOTH category defaults: SaaS blue and DJ neon-purple gradients are forbidden.
- **Type:** one wide grotesque sans for display (gig-flyer energy, caps used sparingly) + one quiet neutral sans for UI and body. Never a third face.
- **Imagery — always:** real venues, real hands on real gear, available light, motion blur welcome.
- **Imagery — never:** stock DJs with glow sticks, laser-neon 3D renders, AI-smooth crowd faces.
- **Reference set:** 12 screenshots in the brand asset folder, sorted yes/no/almost, each "almost" annotated (per PLAYBOOK visual step 1).

**Distinctive asset bet:** the orange lock mark + the one-word confirmation "Locked." — used at every confirmation moment across product, email, and social. Repeat until sick of it, then keep repeating.

## Taglines

1. **"Booked. Signed. Paid."** ← RECOMMENDED — the promise compressed to three words; a stranger can guess what we sell.
2. "Turn DMs into deposits." — strong for ads; slightly narrower than the brand.
3. "The gig is locked." — pairs with the distinctive asset; weaker alone.
4. "Play sets, not billing chase." — rejected: "billing chase" needs explaining.
5. "Empowering DJs everywhere." — rejected on kill-list grounds (empower + everyone).

## Message hierarchy (what gets said first, on every surface)

1. **The promise, compressed** — "Booked. Signed. Paid." / "Every gig signed and deposit-paid before it's confirmed."
2. **The mechanism** — "One booking link with the contract and deposit built in."
3. **The proof** — "€X in deposits collected" / a named DJ's one-line quote / the auto-release rule stated plainly.

Applied:
- Homepage hero: "Booked. Signed. Paid." + sub "Send one link. Get a signed agreement and the deposit before you confirm the date."
- Instagram bio: "Turn DMs into deposits. Booking links with contract + deposit built in."
- App store one-liner: "Booking, contracts, and deposits for working DJs."
- Email signature: "GigDock — Booked. Signed. Paid."
- Support reply footer: mechanism only, no tagline (support context = no marketing per tone flex).

Never lead with features (calendar sync, PDF export) or with the company. Feature copy lives below the fold, always.

## Why this works

- **The alternative is a habit, not a competitor** (PLAYBOOK principle 1). The positioning targets "DMs plus a notes app" because that is what the audience actually does. That's also why the swap test passes against Wave/PayPal: the difference is category-specific behavior — hold a date, chase a deposit, auto-release — not "easier invoicing."
- **The insight names a scene, not a statistic** ("quoting a price and watching the client go quiet"). A scene gives copywriters and image models a concrete moment to build from; "DJs aged 22–35" gives them nothing. This is what RUBRIC D1 = 4 looks like.
- **The NOT-for line is doing strategy work** (principle 2, gate G3). Excluding managed touring artists keeps the copy from drifting toward industry-speak that would alienate the actual buyer.
- **The promise is falsifiable and slightly scary** — "or the date auto-releases" names a consequence the company must engineer around. If the breach were unimaginable, it would be decoration (PLAYBOOK strategy step 7, gate G6). Note reason-to-believe #2 is the same mechanism: the proof and the promise interlock.
- **Every trait has a "but not" boundary plus a behavior**, which is what makes "straight about money" usable inside a refund email at 11pm — not just in a deck (gate G4).
- **Voice rules are countable** — word bans, a 16-word sentence cap, an exclamation-mark ban with named contexts. A mid-tier model and a junior human applying these produce the same brand twice (RUBRIC D4). "Sound confident" would not survive contact with either.
- **Humor gets a jurisdiction, not a volume knob** ("marketing only, gig life only, never the client"). That is how tone flexes while voice stays one person — the flex table changes dials, never personality.
- **The color stance defects from both inherited categories on purpose and says so.** Complying or defecting from the category default is a decision either way; unexamined compliance is how every fintech ends up blue (PLAYBOOK visual step 3).
- **The accent color has a job** (money and confirmation moments), which turns a palette choice into a behavioral system — and combined with the lock mark and "Locked.", it forms one ownable, repeatable asset (RUBRIC D7 = 4).
- **A rejected tagline is shown with its kill-list reason.** Showing the rejection teaches the filter; silent deletion doesn't.
- **Message hierarchy prevents the most common drift** — feature-first copy. With promise → mechanism → proof written down, a mid-tier model writing a landing page cannot accidentally lead with "calendar sync."
- **The name-screen line in the snapshot is dated and marked "(verify current availability)"** — screens go stale; the date tells the next reader whether to trust or re-run them (gate G7).
