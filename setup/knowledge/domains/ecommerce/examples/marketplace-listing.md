| Role | Term | Source |

Mined objections from 3 competitors' reviews (exact buyer phrases):
1. "grit at the bottom of every cup" — filtration complaints dominate 1–2 star reviews.
2. "lukewarm by the second cup" — glass presses lose heat fast.
3. "the handle gets too hot to hold."
4. "carafe shattered in the sink after two weeks."

> Why this step exists: bullets 1–4 below each answer one of these verbatim complaints
> (principle 7: customers write your best copy). We never copied a competitor's sentence —
> we mined their reviews instead (junior mistake #4).

## Title

```
Brewline French Press Coffee Maker 34 oz – Double-Wall Insulated Stainless Steel,
4-Level Filtration, Keeps Coffee Hot 60+ Min – Rust-Proof 304 Steel, Dishwasher
Safe, Brushed Silver
```

Annotations:
- First 80 characters = "Brewline French Press Coffee Maker 34 oz – Double-Wall Insulated Stainless Steel" — a complete, accurate product description on its own. That is the mobile tile (principle 2).
- Primary keyword phrase ("French Press Coffee Maker") sits in words 2–5; brand leads because it's short — if the brand had zero search volume and 3 words, it would still lead on Amazon (style guide) but be trimmed elsewhere.
- Size ("34 oz") is in the first 80 because size is a filter-level buying criterion for this category.
- Every phrase after the size is a harvested secondary keyword doubling as a real attribute — nothing decorative.
- No "best", no "premium", no promo words (RUBRIC G2). ~185 chars total — fits a 200-char category cap; if the category caps at 150, trim from the tail, never the front (verify current category style guide).

## Bullets

**1. STAYS HOT FOR THE SECOND CUP: Double-wall vacuum insulation holds your brew above 140°F for 60+ minutes — glass presses drop to lukewarm in about 20 — while the outside stays cool enough to grab.**
> Job: primary benefit + differentiator. Answers mined objections #2 and #3 in one bullet, with numbers instead of "keeps coffee hot longer" (RUBRIC D4: every claim carries a number or mechanism).

**2. SIZED FOR TWO REAL MUGS: 34 oz / 1 L brews two full 16 oz mugs before a refill. Measures 9.1" H × 6.7" W including handle — fits under standard kitchen cabinets.**
> Job: fit. Converts "34 oz" into what buyers actually picture (mugs), and pre-answers the two size questions that otherwise become returns or "item not as described" (RUBRIC G4).

**3. NO GRIT IN THE LAST SIP: 4-level filtration — dual-layer mesh plus a spring-loaded spiral seal that presses against the wall — keeps grounds out without paper filters.**
> Job: quality proof. Directly answers mined objection #1 using the buyer's own framing ("grit"), and explains the MECHANISM instead of claiming "superior filtration".

**4. ONE PRESS, EVERYWHERE: Hot French press at home, cold brew overnight, loose-leaf tea, and it shrugs off the campsite — no glass to shatter in a sink or a backpack.**
> Job: use cases. Three concrete scenarios widen the buyer pool (tea drinkers, campers, cold-brew) and answer mined objection #4. Each scenario is also a search term this listing now genuinely earns.

**5. IN THE BOX, ON US IF IT FAILS: Press, 2 spare mesh screens, stainless measuring scoop, 1-page brew guide. 12-month replacement guarantee — one email and we ship a new one.**
> Job: risk reversal. Spare screens answer the category's known wear-part anxiety; the guarantee is stated with its actual terms (RUBRIC kill list: no undefined "guarantee").

## Description

```
Your French press makes great coffee for about twenty minutes. Then the glass gives
up, the second cup is lukewarm, and the last sip is half grounds.

The Brewline 34 oz fixes the three things people actually complain about. Double-wall
304 stainless keeps the brew above 140°F for an hour and the outside cool to the touch.
The 4-level filter — two mesh layers plus a spring-loaded spiral seal — leaves the
grit in the pot, not your cup. And there is no glass anywhere, so the sink, the
dishwasher, and the campsite are all safe territory.

SPECS
- Capacity: 34 oz / 1 L (two 16 oz mugs)
- Body: kitchen-grade 304 stainless steel, double-wall vacuum insulated
- Size: 9.1" H × 6.7" W incl. handle · Weight: 2.1 lb
- Care: dishwasher safe; screens rinse clean

IN THE BOX
Press · 2 spare mesh screens · stainless scoop · brew guide
12-month replacement guarantee — email support and we ship a replacement.
```

Annotations:
- Block 1 = the buyer's situation in their own review language (playbook §1 description block 1). It sells by recognition, not by adjectives.
- Block 2 expands the differentiators already promised in bullets — same claims, same numbers; the description never introduces a claim the bullets and images can't back (consistency reads as trust).
- Specs as a scannable list, not prose — no paragraph exceeds 3 lines (RUBRIC D3).
- If brand-registered on Amazon, this becomes A+ modules: banner (block 1 line), 3-icon trio (heat / filter / no-glass), comparison table vs "glass press" generic — keep this text as the fallback.

## Backend search keywords

```
cafetiere coffe plunger pot percolator alternative prensa francesa kaffeebereiter
8 cup single carafe travel brewer gift for dad husband boyfriend housewarming
barista grade 1 liter
```

Included on purpose:
- "cafetiere", "prensa francesa", "kaffeebereiter" — regional/foreign names for the same object; real search volume, zero title space cost.
- "coffe" — top misspelling.
- "8 cup", "1 liter" — the size written the OTHER ways buyers type it (title/bullets have "34 oz" and "1 L").
- Gift terms — this category gets heavy Q4 gift search; gifting stays out of the bullets (kill list: "perfect gift" bullet) but earns backend space.

Excluded on purpose (this list is the method):
- "bodum", "stanley" — competitor brand names: policy violation (RUBRIC G8), even though volume is high.
- "cold brew", "loose leaf tea", "camping" — already indexed via bullet 4; duplicates waste the byte budget.
- "best french press 2026" — subjective + dated term; policy-risky and rots.

## Image shot list

1. Main: press alone on pure white, 3/4 angle, ≥85% of frame, no props/text. (The CTR lever — principle 2; also RUBRIC G6 compliance.)
2. Scale: hand pouring into a standard mug. (Buyers can't judge "34 oz"; hands they can judge.)
3. Lifestyle: on a kitchen counter, morning light, two filled mugs. (Ownership picture for the primary buyer.)
4. Infographic: dimensions overlaid + "140°F at 60 min" heat-retention bar vs glass. (Mobile buyers skim images, not bullets — bullet 1 and 2's numbers must exist as pixels.)
5. Close-up: the spiral-seal filter, disassembled next to the mesh layers. (Proof for bullet 3's mechanism.)
6. In-the-box flat lay: press, 2 screens, scoop, guide. (Backs bullet 5; kills "what's included" questions.)
7. UGC-style: campsite pour-over-the-tailgate shot. (Backs bullet 4's second audience without renting the main image to a niche.)

## Why this works

- Every element traces to harvested data: the title is assembled from ranked keywords, the bullets from mined objections, the images from the bullets. Nothing was written "from imagination" — that's the difference between senior and junior listings (principles 7, 8).
- The tile (image 1 + first 80 chars + price) works standalone; everything else assumes the click already happened (principle 2).
- Claims are mechanistic and numeric — "spring-loaded spiral seal", "140°F for 60+ min" — which is both more persuasive and policy-safe (RUBRIC D4, G3).
- Bullet order follows the playbook's job sequence, so a skimmer who reads only bullet 1 gets the differentiator, and one who reads two gets fit — matching how attention actually decays down a PDP.
- Backend keywords contain zero duplicates of indexed words — the byte budget buys only NEW query coverage.
