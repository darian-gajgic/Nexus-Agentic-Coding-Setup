# Exemplar: Promo Campaign Plan — Brewline Gift Bundle Event (BFCM, own shop)

PLAYBOOK §4 executed as a full plan: goal, unit economics, offer design with rejected
alternatives, day-by-day calendar, asset list, measurement with stop-rules. The core
move: a bundle-led event instead of a sitewide discount — the math below is why.

## 1. Goal (write this before any creative exists)

Primary: contribution-margin dollars, not revenue. Revenue targets hide margin burn.

| KPI | Baseline (same 14 days, no promo) | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $4,200 | $12,000 |
| CM dollars | $2,250 | $5,400 |
| AOV | $41 | $58 |
| Bundle share of promo revenue | — | ≥55% |
| New email subscribers | 90 | +600 |
| Blended MER (revenue ÷ total ad spend) | 4.1 | ≥3.0 floor |

Strategic goals worth naming (PLAYBOOK §4 required-lift rule, clause b): Q4 gift demand
capture, list growth for Q1 lifecycle revenue, AOV habit via threshold.

## 2. Margin math (the part juniors skip — never skip it)

Unit economics, own shop (illustrative; payment fee 2.9% + $0.30 — verify):

| | Press $34.99 | Kettle $44.99 | **Bundle $64.99** |
|---|---|---|---|
| List price basis | 34.99 | 44.99 | 79.98 → 64.99 (−18.7%) |
| Landed COGS | 8.20 | 11.80 | 20.00 |
| Pick/pack/ship | 6.40 | 6.90 | **8.90 (one box)** |
| Payment fee | 1.32 | 1.60 | 2.18 |
| Gift box | — | — | 1.40 |
| **CM per order** | **$19.07 (54.5%)** | **$24.69 (54.9%)** | **$32.51 (50.0%)** |

Read the table like a senior:
- The bundle shows the buyer an $14.99 saving, but combined shipping quietly funds
  $4.40 of it and the two-product order carries more absolute margin than any single
  item. CM% dips 5 points; **CM dollars per order rise 32–70%.** That asymmetry is the
  whole trick: discount depth the customer feels, margin cost we mostly don't.

**Rejected alternative — sitewide 20% off** (do this math every time, in writing):
Press at $27.99 → CM = 27.99 − 8.20 − 6.40 − 1.11 = $12.28 (43.9%).
Required lift = 19.07 ÷ 12.28 = **1.55× units on EVERY order — including the ~40% of
BFCM buyers who would have paid full price anyway.** Last year's data shows ~1.3×
achievable. The math fails, so the offer dies here, before creative. (PLAYBOOK §4.)

Compliance gates: regular prices held ≥30 days before the strike-through (RUBRIC kill
list; legal in many markets — verify locally). End date is real and enforced (principle:
credibility compounds).

## 3. Offer structure (layered, no blanket discount)

1. **Hero: Gift Bundle — press + kettle + gift box, $64.99 (list $79.98).** One clear
   flagship offer; everything else supports it.
2. **Free shipping ≥ $50** (event-long). Set at ~1.3× the $41 AOV — it exists to pull
   a second item into single-product carts.
3. **Gift with purchase ≥ $85:** branded stainless scoop (COGS $1.10, listed value $12).
   Value-add instead of deeper %-off — protects price integrity on the upper tier.
4. **VIP early access:** email list shops 24h early. The privilege is ACCESS, not an
   extra discount — rewards the list without training discount-waiting.
5. Single-use discount codes per channel (email/social) — blocks coupon-site leakage
   and gives per-channel attribution for free.

## 5. Asset list (each asset: one job, one CTA — brief against STYLE-VOICE.md)

Email (6):
1. VIP early access — subject "You shop 24 hours before everyone else" — job: reward + first sales.
2. Public launch — "The Brewline gift bundle exists now" — job: announce hero offer, one CTA to bundle PDP.
3. Social proof — "23 people already rated this 4.7" — job: convert the hesitant with reviews/UGC.
4. Gift angle — "One gift, two very good mornings" — job: gift buyers, shipping-deadline reassurance.
5. Last 48h — "Bundle price ends Sunday midnight" — job: deadline, real countdown.
6. Post-event — buyers: care guide; non-buyers: brew content — job: relationship reset, no selling.

Social (8): 3 static (bundle flat-lay, margin-safe price badge, review quote card),
3 stories (unboxing the gift box, poll "press or kettle first?", countdown final day),
2 short video (30s pour ritual UGC style, gift-wrap close-up). All link with channel code.

Site: hero banner (bundle only — one message), announcement bar (threshold + deadline),
PDP badges on both single products ("Better in the bundle — save $14.99"), cart module
("Add the kettle, unlock the bundle price"), post-purchase upsell page.

Support: macros for code-not-working / ship-deadline / can-I-swap-bundle-items.

## 6. Measurement

Daily during event (10 min): revenue, orders, AOV, bundle share, code redemptions by
channel, ad MER, inventory cover per SKU.

Stop-rules and pivots (decide these BEFORE launch, execute without debate):
1. Blended MER < 3.0 for 2 consecutive days → cut paid spend 50%, keep email/organic.
2. Bundle share < 35% at T+5 → swap hero creative to bundle-only, move singles below fold.
3. Any SKU hits {{FILL: safety stock units}} → pull that SKU's ads + hero, protect the
   bundle; a stockout mid-event turns rank and momentum into a cliff (principle 10).
4. Code leaked to coupon aggregators → kill code, reissue segmented codes (why they were
   single-use per channel).

Post-mortem at T+17, answer in writing:
1. CM dollars vs target (the number that matters) — and vs the no-promo baseline.
2. Required-lift check: did bundle CM math hold after real shipping costs?
3. Which email/channel drove margin, not clicks (single-use codes answer this).
4. New-subscriber 60-day value: watch the Q1 lifecycle revenue before calling list
   growth a win.
5. One thing to cut next time, one to double. → WINS.md / LESSONS.md.

## Why this works

- The offer was designed backwards from margin math, and one alternative was killed BY
  the math in writing — that habit, more than any tactic, is what separates senior promo
  planning (principle 4; PLAYBOOK §4 required-lift rule).
- Bundle + threshold + GWP all spend less margin than their perceived value: combined
  shipping, AOV lift, and a $1.10 gift doing $12 of persuasion. Discounts customers FEEL
  but the P&L barely does.
- Access (VIP window) rewards loyalty without a deeper price cut — no discount-training,
  no reference-price damage (PLAYBOOK §4 psychology rules 1, 4).
- Deadlines, inventory claims, and strike-throughs are all literally true — urgency that
  survives a screenshot keeps working next year (kill list: fake urgency).
- Stop-rules were pre-committed, so mid-event decisions are execution, not panic.
