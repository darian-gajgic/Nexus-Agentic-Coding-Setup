# Email launch sequence exemplar — 5 emails ("Brieflow" launch)

Illustrative scenario: launching **Brieflow** ($29/mo SaaS, see `landing-page.md`) to an owned list of ~1,800 freelance marketers built via the newsletter. Launch offer: **30% off the first year + a 1:1 setup call, for the first 100 customers or until Friday 23:59 — whichever comes first.** The deadline is real and enforced (RUBRIC: fake urgency is a kill-list item).

Arc: tease → value → launch → objections/social proof → last call. Timing: Tue / Thu / Tue / Thu / Fri.
Suppression rule (binary): anyone who buys leaves the sequence immediately. Emails 4–5 go only to non-purchasers.

**What does client reporting look like for you right now?** (Tool, spreadsheet, "I write it from scratch at 9pm" — one line is plenty.)

## EMAIL 2 — VALUE (Thursday, Day -5)

**Job:** give something genuinely useful with no gate — earn attention before spending it. Mention the launch once, softly.

**Subject options:**
- A: steal my client report template
- B: the 5-section report that renews retainers
- C: what clients actually read in your reports

**Preview text:** Copy it, use it Friday, no signup, no catch.

**Body:**

Whatever tool you use — even if it's a Google Doc at 9pm — the reports that keep clients paying share one structure. Here's ours; steal it:

1. **The headline number.** One metric, moving the direction the client cares about, top of page. Everything else is context.
2. **What we did.** 3–5 bullets, past tense, specific ("rewrote 4 ad hooks", not "optimized campaigns").
3. **What we learned.** One insight. This is the section clients quote back — it's the part that sounds like a consultant, not a vendor.
4. **What's next.** 3 bullets. Sets the agenda before they set it for you.
5. **What we need from you.** One ask, with a deadline. A report without an ask trains clients that reports need no answer.

Why this order works: clients don't read reports, they scan for "are we okay and is someone driving?" Sections 1 and 4 answer that in 20 seconds. The rest is evidence.

## Why this works

- **The arc sells before it asks:** E1 buys attention with a story and a question, E2 pays the reader before charging them, E3 asks once and completely, E4 removes friction, E5 forces the decision. One job per email (PLAYBOOK §3.1) — no email does two things.
- **E1's reply question is load-bearing:** replies train inbox providers that this sender is wanted (deliverability), and the answers become E4's objection list verbatim (Principle 3: copy is assembled). This is research disguised as engagement.
- **E2 gives the template ungated.** Reciprocity plus demonstrated expertise: the reader who uses the template Friday experiences the pain the product solves, with our structure in their hands. The soft P.S. is the only pitch — trust compounds on the owned channel (Principle 4).
- **E3 states the entire offer in one screen:** what, proof, price, deadline, risk reversal, one button. Bullets carry numbers, not adjectives (Principle 5). Testimonial has a name and a number (RUBRIC gate 3/6).
- **The urgency is real and mechanically explained** ("first 100 or Friday, whichever comes first", "not a countdown that resets") — fake urgency is a kill-list offense and, worse, it trains the list to ignore deadlines.
- **E4's "who it's NOT for" and "if the math doesn't clear, don't buy"** are trust purchases. Disqualifying honestly makes the qualifying claims believable and cuts refunds/churn later.
- **"62 of 100 gone" is a specific, checkable scarcity number** — only usable because it's true. If it weren't tracked, the line would be cut, not rounded.
- **E5 adds zero new arguments and is the shortest email.** By last-call day, remaining non-buyers need a deadline and a door. Loss framing (what disappears at midnight) does the work. "Last launch email either way" respects the list — that's why the next launch works too.
- **Suppression discipline** (buyers exit instantly) — nothing burns goodwill like a discount pitch after purchase. This is a binary send-check, not a nicety.
- **Subjects are 24–45 chars, lowercase-leaning, specific; preview text extends rather than repeats** (PLAYBOOK §3.2). Every deadline states a timezone.
