# SEO content brief exemplar — "client reporting tools" ("Brieflow" blog)

Illustrative scenario: **Brieflow** (see `landing-page.md`) targeting a commercial BOFU-adjacent keyword per PLAYBOOK §5.2. This is the brief a writer (human or agent) receives — complete enough that no strategic decision is left to the drafting step.

---

## THE BRIEF

### 1. Target

- **Primary keyword:** client reporting tools — ~1,300 searches/mo US (illustrative; pull real volume before writing — verify current numbers)
- **Secondary keywords to weave in naturally:** client reporting software (~900/mo), automated client reports, marketing reporting tools for agencies, client report template
- **URL:** `/blog/client-reporting-tools` — keep flat, no date in slug
- **Content type:** listicle/comparison post on the blog. NOT a product landing page — see intent analysis.
- **Business goal:** assisted trials. Every internal CTA carries `?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=202607-reporting-tools` per PLAYBOOK §6.1.

### 2. Intent analysis (the SERP is the answer key — PLAYBOOK §5.1)

Googled 2026-07 (re-check at writing time; SERPs move):

- Top 10 = 8 listicles ("12 best client reporting tools"), 1 vendor comparison page, 1 Reddit thread ranking #7.
- **Verdict: commercial investigation.** Searcher is compiling a shortlist, not ready to buy one tool and not asking "what is client reporting". A product page cannot rank here; a guide-only post won't satisfy. Format must be: comparison listicle with a decision aid.
- The Reddit thread in the top 10 = difficulty green light (PLAYBOOK §5.3): the SERP is beatable without DR-70 authority.
- People Also Ask: "What is the best free client reporting tool?", "How do I automate client reports?", "What should a client report include?" — each becomes a section or FAQ below.
- Snapshot the SERP into the brief (paste the top-10 titles + date): the gap analysis below must stay auditable after the SERP moves.

### 3. SERP gap (what the top 10 miss — our angle)

1. Every list targets 15–50 person agencies; **nobody segments for freelancers/sub-10 teams** — pricing tables start at enterprise tiers.
2. **Pricing info is stale or missing** in 6 of 10 results (verify by checking each tool's live pricing page — cite the check date in the article).
3. **No setup-time estimates anywhere** — yet "how long to set up" is the #1 presale question in our support inbox.
4. No result answers the free-tool PAA properly (they list trials as "free").
5. Screenshots are vendor press images, not real reports.

**Angle (title promise): the only list segmented by team size, with live-checked pricing and real setup times.** Written by someone who runs client accounts. We include Brieflow, disclosed, not #1-by-default — see E-E-A-T notes.

### 4. Outline (H2/H3 with per-section notes)

- **[Intro, 60–90 words]** Direct answer up top (PLAYBOOK §5.10): "The best client reporting tool for most freelancers and small agencies is [X] — here are 8 options compared by price, setup time, and integrations, checked [month year]." One line on method. No throat-clearing about "today's data-driven world" (kill list).
- **H2: Comparison table — all 8 tools at a glance**
  Columns: tool, best for (team size), price from, free tier?, setup time, key integrations. This table is the featured-snippet and AI-answer bait — it must stand alone (PLAYBOOK §5.10).
- **H2: How we picked (and who this list is for)**
  50–80 words: tested with real client accounts, live pricing checked on [date], written for sub-10-person teams. Disclosure sentence: "We make Brieflow, one of the tools below — we've marked it and kept the comparison honest."
- **H2: The 8 best client reporting tools for small teams** — one H3 per tool, 150–220 words each, identical structure so the post scans (RUBRIC dim. 6): what it is (1 line) → best for → standout feature → limitation (mandatory — a list with no cons reads as pay-to-play) → price (live-checked) → setup time → real screenshot.

- **H2: The best free client reporting tool** — answers the PAA head-on: Looker Studio, honestly, with its trade-offs (setup hours, no scheduling polish). Answering "free" honestly when we sell a paid tool is a trust play the vendor listicles refuse to make — that's why it wins the snippet.
- **H2: How to choose in 10 minutes** — decision aid: 3 questions (how many clients, which data sources, weekly or monthly cadence) mapping to a recommendation. This is the section that makes shortlist-compilers stop searching.

- **H2: FAQ** — remaining PAA verbatim as H3 questions, 40–60 word answers each (answer-shaped for AI overviews, PLAYBOOK §5.10):

  - H3: How much do client reporting tools cost?

### 6. Internal links

- **Out (this post → money pages, PLAYBOOK §5.7):** Brieflow features page (anchor: "automated client reports"), pricing page (anchor: "Brieflow pricing"), the client-report-template post (anchor: "client report template").
- **In (edit same day):** from the template post (anchor: "client reporting tools"), from the "agency workflow" post, and from the integrations index. Zero-orphan rule applies.
- **CTA placement:** one contextual CTA after the Brieflow H3, one end-of-post banner. Not more — this is a trust asset first.

### 7. Title & meta options

Titles (≤ 60 chars, front-loaded keyword, CTR element — pick after checking live SERP for duplication):
1. `8 Best Client Reporting Tools for Small Teams (2026)` — 53 chars
2. `Client Reporting Tools Compared: Prices + Setup Time` — 52 chars
3. `The 8 Best Client Reporting Tools (Freelancer Guide)` — 52 chars

Meta descriptions (≤ 155 chars, contains keyword + the differentiator + soft CTA):
1. `We compared 8 client reporting tools on live pricing, setup time, and integrations — built for freelancers and agencies under 10 people.` — 136 chars
2. `The best client reporting tools for small teams, tested with real client accounts. Prices checked July 2026, setup times included.` — 130 chars

### 8. Length, format, refresh

- **Word count: 2,600–3,200.** Rationale: top-3 results run 2,400 / 3,100 / 2,900 — match the SERP median, don't inflate; padding past the median adds decay risk, not rank (PLAYBOOK §5: comprehensiveness wins, length doesn't).
- Format requirements: comparison table within the first 2 scrolls; real screenshots (our own report exports where possible); visible "last checked" date next to every price; author byline with practitioner bio.
- Structured data: FAQ schema on the FAQ block, ItemList schema on the tool list (verify current rich-result eligibility — Google trims these periodically).
- Media: compress images to < 150KB; alt text is descriptive and carries entity names naturally (accessibility first, image SEO second).
- **Refresh cadence: quarterly** — pricing rows rot fastest; a stale price in a comparison post kills the post's trust and its rankings. Add to the refresh calendar at publish time.

### 9. Success criteria (set before publishing, PLAYBOOK §6.6)

- Rank: top 10 in 60 days, top 5 in 120 days (verify realistic against difficulty at writing time; if not met at 120 days → refresh with gap re-analysis, don't write a duplicate post).
- Business: ≥ 10 UTM-attributed trial starts/month by month 4 (illustrative — set from BUSINESS-CONTEXT.md funnel numbers).
- Guardrail: zero fabricated prices/claims — every factual row carries a check date (RUBRIC gate 6).

### 10. Distribution (first 72 hours — a post without links needs a push to start ranking)

1. Feature in the newsletter with a one-insight teaser (not "new blog post!").
2. Post the comparison table's key finding natively in 1–2 communities where we're already contributing members; link only if asked or allowed. Drive-by link drops get removed and burn the account.
3. Email the 7 featured tools: "we compared you here — happy to fix anything we got wrong." Some share it; occasionally one links it. Corrections improve the post either way.
4. Verify internal links from section 6 went live the same day; request indexing in Search Console.
5. Log the URL + target keyword in the rank tracker and the quarterly refresh calendar.

### 11. Writer instructions (drafting rules — non-negotiable)

- Voice per `~/knowledge/STYLE-VOICE.md`; first-person practitioner ("we tested", "in our accounts"), never tourist voice ("many agencies find that…").
- Every price, count, and claim carries an inline check date; no statistic without a source. Anything unverifiable gets cut, not hedged (RUBRIC gate 6).
- Kill-list sweep before handoff (RUBRIC); reading level ≤ grade 9.
- Screenshots: our own exports, consistent width, captioned. No vendor press images.
- Do NOT: pad past the word target, editorialize about "the importance of reporting", bury the table below tool sections, or soften the Brieflow limitation.

## Why this works

- **Format is dictated by the SERP, not by preference** (PLAYBOOK §5.1). Eight listicles ranking means Google has declared the answer format; publishing a product page or a think-piece here loses before writing begins. Juniors: always write down the SERP verdict — it ends most "what should we write" debates.
- **The keyword is BOFU-adjacent commercial** (PLAYBOOK §5.2): shortlist-compilers are weeks, not years, from buying. 1,300/mo with purchase intent beats 40,000/mo "marketing reports" traffic that never converts.
- **The Reddit-thread-in-top-10 observation is the difficulty check done for free** (PLAYBOOK §5.3) — it's the documented reason this brief exists instead of being skipped.
- **The angle comes from named gaps, not ambition.** "Segmented by team size + live-checked pricing + setup times" are three checkable deficits in the current top 10 — each maps to a feature of our post. An angle you can't trace to a SERP gap is a guess.
- **Disclosure + mandatory limitations per tool** are E-E-A-T load-bearing: a vendor listicle that hides its bias gets outranked by the first honest one, and readers convert better from a source that conceded something (same principle as the landing page's "but my reports are custom" section).
- **Answering "best free tool" honestly (Looker Studio)** targets the PAA snippet and buys the credibility that makes the paid recommendation land. Refusing to name free alternatives is the classic vendor tell.
- **Identical H3 structure per tool** makes the post scannable (RUBRIC dim. 6) and makes future quarterly refreshes mechanical — update rows, not prose.
- **Direct-answer intro + standalone table + verbatim-question FAQ** are the AI-overview/answer-engine surface (PLAYBOOK §5.10) — in 2026 the table is as likely to be cited by an LLM as clicked in blue links.
- **Word count is evidence-based** (SERP median), killing the junior default of "longer ranks better".
- **Success criteria + refresh cadence are in the brief**, so the post is a managed asset with a review date, not a publish-and-forget (PLAYBOOK §6.6).
- **Distribution lives in the brief** because rankings need initial links and engagement signals — "publish and wait" is why most small-site content dies at position 40. The tools-featured outreach is the quiet link-earner: vendors love being compared well.
- **Writer instructions make the brief executable by a mid-tier agent:** the strategic thinking (sections 1–3) is locked before drafting, so the drafting step can't silently change the strategy — the same reason campaign strategy runs objective-first (PLAYBOOK §1).
