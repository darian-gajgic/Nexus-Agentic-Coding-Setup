## Why this works

- **Headline = outcome + moment, ≤ 12 words** (PLAYBOOK §2.3). "Before they ask" targets the emotional trigger (client chasing you), not the feature (report automation). Specific ritual words ("every Friday") beat abstract ones ("automatically").
- **Subhead does the qualifying.** Naming the exact tools (Analytics, Meta) lets the right visitor self-identify in 3 seconds and bounces the wrong one cheaply — a filtered click is a feature, not a loss.
- **CTA microcopy answers the two peak anxieties at the button:** cost ("no card") and effort ("15 minutes"). Objections get answered at the moment they occur, not two screens later.
- **Hero image shows the artifact, not the dashboard.** Buyers imagine sending the report; nobody fantasizes about software UI. Show the thing they'll show others.
- **Social proof sits immediately under the hero** because an unknown brand's biggest unstated objection is "is this real?" — settle it before asking for attention (RUBRIC dimension 3).
- **Problem section is assembled voice-of-customer** (Principle 3): "Friday, 6:40pm", "every tab open", "quick question — where are we" are the kind of verbatim details only research produces. The line "proving you did marketing" reframes the pain sharper than the reader would — that's the agitation.
- **Each benefit block runs feature → so what → proof** (PLAYBOOK §2.5–2.6): headline is the benefit, body is the mechanism, and a proof line is adjacent — no claim floats unproven (RUBRIC gate 3). Note every proof contains a number or a name.
- **"How it works" in 3 numbered steps** kills the top silent objection for any tool ("setup will eat a day"). Effort is stated in numbers, twice on the page.
- **Objection section headline is the objection verbatim, in quotes.** Naming the reader's exact thought buys more trust than any claim. The answer concedes ground honestly ("you don't stop thinking") — honesty is a conversion tactic.
- **Guarantee is concrete, not "risk-free":** it names what happens if it fails (export your data and template). Specific mechanisms are believed; adjectives are not (Principle 5).
- **One CTA, repeated 3× down the page, never varied in action** (RUBRIC gate 2); final CTA restates promise + effort + risk and adds nothing new — decided readers need a door, not another argument.
- **Nav removed, footer stripped:** one page, one job (PLAYBOOK §2.2). Every exit link is a leak on a campaign page.
- **Kill-list clean:** no "seamless", no "revolutionize", no exclamation marks; you/your outnumbers we/our.
- **Every number on the page is defensible:** 15 minutes, 3.1 hours, 1,400 users, month 19 — each traces to workspace data or a named person. That's RUBRIC gate 6 doing sales work: checkable numbers read as confidence.
- **The page argues in the reader's objection order,** not our feature order: is it real → is it my problem → what do I get → is it hard → who says so → what's the catch → remaining questions → door. Reorder sections only if research shows a different objection order.
