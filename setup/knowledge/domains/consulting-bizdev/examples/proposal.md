## 1. Where you are now

From our call on 2 July — in your words:

- "The store feels slow even on good wifi, and mobile is where it hurts."
- "We redesigned last year and conversion never really recovered."
- Roughly 60,000 sessions/month, ~68% on mobile.
- Desktop converts at ~2.6%; mobile at ~1.1%. Average order value ~€85.
- Your bestseller page (Alpine 40) measures 5.8s LCP on mobile; 14 apps installed, several loading before the product renders.

The gap between desktop and mobile conversion is the headline. Closing even a tenth
of it is worth roughly €5,000/month at your current traffic — that's the number this
project is judged against.

## 2. What success looks like

1. Mobile pages fast enough that speed stops being a plausible reason to bounce (LCP ≤ 2.5s on the money pages).
2. A clear, prioritized picture of every conversion blocker we find on the way — not just speed.
3. Your team able to keep it fast: you know exactly what changed and what to stop reinstalling.

## 3. How we'll get there

Phase 1 — Audit (days 1–4)
- Full speed + conversion pass on the five money templates: home, collection, product, cart, checkout entry.
- Every issue logged with: expected impact, effort, and who can fix it (us / your team / app vendor).
- Day-4 checkpoint call (30 min): we walk the register together and lock the top-10 fix list. **This is also your exit point — see Terms.**

Phase 2 — Fix (days 5–9)
- We implement the locked top-10 on a duplicated theme. Nothing touches the live store without your written OK.
- Anything a fix can't solve (e.g. an app vendor's script) is documented with the exact cause and your options.

Phase 3 — Handover (day 10)
- Before/after measurement on all five templates, same tooling as the audit.
- 15-minute recorded walkthrough of everything changed, plus a "don't undo this" list.

## 4. Deliverables

1. Issue register: every issue found, with impact estimate, effort, and owner (typically 15–25 items).
2. Top-10 fixes implemented and live (post-approval), on a rollback-safe duplicated theme.
3. Before/after metric sheet: LCP, total page weight, request count — per template, dated.
4. Recorded walkthrough video + "don't undo this" maintenance list.
5. 14-day warranty: anything we shipped that breaks, we fix free.

## Delivering this proposal — the 15-minute walkthrough (script)

1. Open: "I'll walk through this in ten minutes, then questions. Nothing in here should surprise you — it's what we discussed, written down."
2. Sections 1–2 slowly (their words, their numbers). Watch for corrections — a correction here is a gift; fix it live and thank them.
3. Sections 3–5 in two minutes. Do not lecture methodology; they're buying the outcome.
4. Section 6: read BOTH prices out loud, B first — then stop talking. Count five seconds. Their first reaction is the negotiation's true starting point.
5. Price pushback → move scope, never price: "We can meet €X by moving [item] out of scope — would you rather?"
6. "We'll discuss internally" → "Of course. What will the discussion hinge on — price, timing, or whether you trust us to deliver? … Who's the discussion with? Want me to join for ten minutes?"
7. Close on the date: "The proposal and the 20 July start hold until the 20th. I'll close it after that either way — you'll always get a straight answer from us."

## Why this works

- **Section 1 is their words, quoted.** The proposal opens as a receipt of the call, not a pitch (Playbook playbook 4, rule zero). "They listened" closes more deals than "they're impressive" — and it's the one thing a junior can always out-deliver an agency on.
- **One number rules the document.** The €5,000/month gap, derived from THEIR numbers on the call, appears in sections 1 and 6. Fee €2,400 vs. value ~€60k/year keeps the price a rounding error against the outcome (Playbook pricing heuristic 1: value ≥ 3x fee, with huge margin).
- **Two options, bigger first.** B anchors A; "which one?" replaces "yes or no?". No third option — a third adds a week of dithering for a deal this size (Playbook playbook 4, steps 4–5).
- **The exit point is the junior's trust weapon.** "Stop at day 4 for €800" converts "can we trust two juniors?" into a capped €800 experiment. Risk-reversal beats credentials when you don't have credentials (RUBRIC dimension 6).
- **Success criteria are leading indicators only.** We commit to LCP, shipped fixes, and documentation — things we control. The explicit "what we don't promise" paragraph reads as the most senior thing in the document; juniors overpromise, professionals bound their promises (Playbook mistake 14).
- **Terms protect the small team without sounding hostile.** Deposit starts the clock; late input shifts dates 1:1; ≥3 named exclusions; swap-or-add for changes; IP on final payment. Every clause maps to Playbook playbook 4, step 8, and each one prevents a classic small-shop death (free scope creep, waiting unpaid on client assets, ransom-free IP handover).
- **The founding-rate case-study trade is named in the terms.** The discount has a stated price — a named case study — which preserves the real rate (Playbook pricing rules) and manufactures the portfolio a junior team needs (principle 10).
- **Expiry and start-date hold are real scarcity.** Dates, not pressure phrases. If it lapses, it lapses — scarcity only works if it's true (Playbook playbook 4, step 10; RUBRIC dimension 8).
- **Next step is a reply word plus a pre-held slot.** The yes takes ten seconds and the walkthrough is already scheduled — no "let us know your thoughts" limbo (RUBRIC P6, dimension 4).
