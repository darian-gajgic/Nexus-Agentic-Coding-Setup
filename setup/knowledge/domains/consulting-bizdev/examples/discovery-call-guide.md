# Discovery Call Guide — 30 minutes, qualify or disqualify

Used when a prospect replies to outreach or arrives by referral. Goal is NOT to sell:
it is to (a) decide if this is a client we want, (b) gather the numbers a proposal
needs, (c) anchor a price bracket, (d) leave with a scheduled next step.
Worked example thread: Jonas Brandt, Kestrel Supply Co. (see the other exemplars).

## Before the call (10 minutes, mandatory)

1. Re-read the outreach thread and homework findings.
2. Check their site, LinkedIn, and anything public that changed since.
3. Write down: one HYPOTHESIS ("mobile speed is costing them ~€5k/mo") and one QUESTION you cannot answer from the outside ("why did conversion not recover after the redesign?").
4. Have the scorecard (below) open for scoring DURING the call.
5. Decide your price bracket for the likely shape of work: {{FILL: bracket per offer from BUSINESS-CONTEXT.md — e.g. "€2,000–€4,000"}}.

## Agenda (30 min)

| Min | Phase |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Frame |
| 2–7 | Situation |
| 7–17 | Problem + impact |
| 17–22 | Money, authority, timeline |
| 22–27 | Fit + price bracket |
| 27–30 | Close to next step |

Talk-time rule: you speak ≤ 30% of minutes 2–22. If you catch yourself explaining
your methods before minute 22, stop and ask the next question.

## Question bank by phase

### Phase 1 — Frame (script, verbatim)

"Thanks for the time — here's how I'd suggest we use 30 minutes. I'll ask about how
things run today and where it hurts; if it looks like we can help, I'll sketch how
we'd approach it and what work like that costs. At the end we both decide if a next
step makes sense — and it's completely fine if the answer is no. Sound OK?"

Why the "fine if no" line: it removes sales pressure, which paradoxically raises
honesty and close rates — and it's a frame a junior can hold without bravado.

### Phase 2 — Situation

- "Walk me through how [the area] works today — who does what?"
- "What made you take this call now, rather than three months ago?"  ← highest-signal question; listen for a trigger event.
- "What have you already tried? What happened?"
- "Who else has this problem touched — support, ops, you personally?"

### Phase 3 — Problem + impact (get numbers or get honesty)

- "Where does it hurt the most, concretely?"
- "How are you measuring that today?" (If unmeasured, that's a finding, not a dead end.)
- "Rough numbers are fine — what do you think this costs per month?"
- If they can't estimate, build it together: "What's monthly traffic? Share on mobile? Conversion on each? Average order?" ← this co-built number becomes the proposal's ruling number.
- "What happens if you do nothing for six months?"  ← surfaces the real competitor: no-decision.
- "If this were fixed by [date], what would that make possible?"

### Phase 4 — Money, authority, timeline (ask directly; wobbling here kills proposals)

- "Have you set aside a budget for this, even a rough one?"
- If no: "No budget ever, or not this quarter? What number would it need to fit inside?"
- "Who besides you needs to say yes?" → if anyone: "What will their yes depend on? Should they join the next call?"
- "When does this need to be live to matter?"

### Phase 5 — Fit + price bracket (the anchor — never skip)

1. Two-sentence approach, no jargon: "Based on what you've said: we'd audit the five money pages first, agree the top-10 fixes with you, then implement them on a safe copy — two weeks end to end."
2. Bracket script, verbatim: "Work like this usually lands between €2,400 and €3,900 depending on whether you want us to stay on for the month after. Is that the world you were imagining?"
3. Read the reaction:
   - Nod / "that's fine" → proceed to close.
   - Silence → hold it. Do not fill the silence, do not discount into it. They're thinking.
   - Flinch / "we were thinking less" → "What number did you have in mind? … We can meet €X by cutting [scope item] — the core stays intact." Scope moves, price integrity stays (Playbook principle 5).
   - Flinch + no number + no flexibility → this is a disqualification signal; score it and close politely.

### Phase 6 — Close to next step (script, verbatim)

Qualified: "This looks like a fit from my side. Next step: I'll send a short proposal —
two options, everything we discussed, nothing new in it. Let's book 15 minutes now to
walk through it together — Thursday or Friday?"  ← book it ON the call.

Unsure (needs internal check on our side): "I want to check one thing about [X] before
promising anything. I'll email you by tomorrow noon either a proposal slot or a straight
'we're not the right fit' — deal?"

Same day, no exceptions: 5-line recap email — their problem in their words, the numbers
they gave, the bracket named, next step + date.

## Qualification scorecard (fill during the call)

Score each 0 / 1 / 2:

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pain | Vague annoyance | Named problem | Named + a trigger event ("why now") |
| Impact | No number, no interest | Rough estimate | Number, co-built or measured |
| Budget | None + no flexibility | "Could find it" for the bracket | Budget exists ≥ bracket floor |
| Authority | Decision-maker absent, won't appear | DM reachable, will join next call | DM on this call |
| Timeline | "Someday" | This quarter | Dated trigger (launch, season, contract) |
| Deliverability | We'd be learning from scratch | Adjacent to proven work | We've done exactly this ≥ once |

Decision thresholds:
- **≥ 8/12 AND no zero on Pain or Budget → proposal** (within 48h, per Playbook playbook 4).
- **5–7 → nurture:** no proposal. Send one useful thing now, set a dated follow-up on their trigger ("you mentioned the autumn line — I'll check back mid-August").
- **≤ 4 → decline within 24h**, politely (script below).
- Any 2+ red flags (below) → decline regardless of score.

Red flags (from Playbook playbook 3): trash-talks 2+ past vendors with no self-reflection;
haggles before scope exists; "should be simple/quick" from their side; requests free spec
work; urgent deadline + vague goal; decision-maker permanently unavailable.

## Objection responses

- "Just send me a price list." → "Fixed prices are exactly how we work — the sprint is €2,400. I just won't take it before 15 minutes confirms it'd actually move your numbers; if it wouldn't, I'll say so and send you the audit checklist instead."
- "Can you do it cheaper?" → "What budget are you working with? … We can hit €X by dropping [item]. The price only moves when the scope does — otherwise you couldn't trust any number I've given you."
- "We tried an agency and it didn't work." → "What did they do, and where did it stall? … Two differences worth testing us on: you get the two people doing the work, and you can stop at day 4 for €800 if the audit doesn't convince you."
- "We need it live in two weeks." → Take it only if genuinely deliverable; otherwise: "Honest answer: done right this takes [real time]. I'd rather lose this than ship you something broken. Here's what IS doable in two weeks: [reduced scope]."
- "Can you also do [ads/SEO/app]?" → "Maybe — but not in this project. One scope, one outcome, then we look at what's next. Mixed projects are how budgets evaporate."
- "I need to think about it." → "Of course. What's the part you need to think over — the price, the approach, or whether it's a priority right now?" ← the answer tells you which objection is real; address that one only.
- "Just email the proposal, no call needed." → "I'll keep the walkthrough to 15 minutes — proposals I just email tend to die in inboxes, and if something's off I'd rather fix it live than guess. If Thursday's bad, I'll trade you: I send it, you reply with three questions."

## Decline script (for ≤ 4 scores or red flags)

"Thanks for the time today. Honest take: I don't think we're the right fit for this —
[one true reason: 'the timeline is tighter than we can do well' / 'this needs a
specialist in X']. So I don't leave you empty-handed: [one pointer — a checklist, a
name, a tool]. If [changed condition] happens, I'd be glad to look again."

One true reason, one gift, one door left open. No fake "we're at capacity."

## Same-day recap email (send within 4 business hours of the call)

Subject: `notes + next step — Kestrel Supply`

Hi Jonas — thanks for today. What I heard, so we're building on the same facts:

- Mobile converts at ~1.1% vs ~2.6% on desktop; ~60k sessions/mo, ~68% mobile, AOV ~€85 — we put the gap at roughly €5k/month.
- Trigger: the autumn line lands mid-September; you want the store fast before traffic peaks.
- Budget: the €2,400–€3,900 bracket we discussed works on your side.
- Next step: proposal walkthrough Thursday 14:00 (invite attached) — two options, nothing in it we didn't discuss.

If I got anything wrong, reply and I'll correct it before it goes into the proposal.

Darian

This email is load-bearing, not politeness: it freezes the facts the proposal will quote
(RUBRIC P8), turns the verbal bracket into a written one (the evidence RUBRIC P7 checks
for), and opens a correction window — corrections before the proposal are free; after it,
they cost the deal.

## Why this works

- **Decide-together frame (Phase 1)** removes the pressure a junior can't carry anyway and reframes the call as mutual evaluation — which is also what qualification actually is (Playbook principle 7).
- **"Why now?" and "what if you do nothing?"** find the trigger event and price the no-decision path. Deals without a trigger stall in "sounds great" forever; these two questions predict close probability better than any enthusiasm on the call.
- **Co-building the impact number (Phase 3)** matters twice: a number the client computed with you is believed, and it becomes the proposal's ruling number (see `proposal.md`, section 1). Never import your own estimate when theirs can be built live.
- **Direct money/authority questions (Phase 4)** feel rude to juniors and read as professional to buyers. Every wobble here becomes a proposal written blind — the #1 cause of ghosted proposals (Playbook mistake 2).
- **The bracket (Phase 5) is the anchor** demanded by Playbook principle 4 and checked by RUBRIC P7. Naming it as a range tied to a scope fork ("depending on whether…") anchors without committing, and the reaction is data you can't get by email.
- **Silence-after-price rule:** the first person to speak after a price names the discount. Juniors discount into silence out of fear; the script makes holding it a procedure instead of a nerve test.
- **The scorecard turns taste into arithmetic.** A junior's judgment under pipeline pressure is unreliable (Playbook mistake 11); "≥ 8/12, no zero on Pain or Budget" is not negotiable with yourself at 6/12.
- **The decline script preserves the asset.** Declined-well prospects refer and return; "at capacity" lies eventually leak. One true reason + one gift costs two minutes and compounds.
