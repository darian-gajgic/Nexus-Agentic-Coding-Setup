# Content Creation Playbook

> Read together with `~/knowledge/BUSINESS-CONTEXT.md` (who we are, what we sell, ICPs) and
> `~/knowledge/STYLE-VOICE.md` (how we sound). This file governs craft and process.
> Quality gate: `RUBRIC.md` in this folder — run it on every piece before publishing.
> Worked examples: `examples/` (long-form article, newsletter issue, repurposing set).

## Operating principles

1. **The hook decides 80% of the outcome.** Write 10 hooks, keep 1. *Why: readers decide in one line; a great body behind a weak hook is unread work.*
2. **One piece = one idea.** If the takeaway needs two sentences, it's two pieces. *Why: people only share what they can retell.*
3. **Lead with the claim, then earn it.** Never warm up to the point. *Why: a stranger grants you about two sentences of trust before scrolling.*
4. **Specificity is credibility.** "Churn fell 22% in 60 days" beats "churn improved dramatically." *Why: adjectives are free; numbers prove you were there.*
5. **Document, don't invent.** Write what we did, measured, or broke this week — not "expert content" assembled from Google. *Why: first-hand experience is a 2-person team's only unfair advantage.*
6. **Create once, distribute 5+ times.** Every pillar piece must yield at least 5 platform-native assets. *Why: creation costs hours, repurposing costs minutes — leverage lives in distribution.*
7. **Never cross-post verbatim.** Rewrite the hook and reformat for each platform. *Why: every platform punishes foreign formats — visibly in reach, invisibly in trust.*
8. **Consistency compounds; virality doesn't.** Ship the calendar with a 7/10 piece rather than skip a week polishing a 9. *Why: audiences reward cadence; one spike changes nothing.*
9. **Match the CTA to reader temperature.** Cold reader → follow; warm → email list; hot → product/call. *Why: asking a stranger to buy burns both the piece and the trust.*
10. **Platform mechanics expire.** Tag any algorithm/feature claim "(verify current specifics)" and re-check before acting on it. *Why: last year's growth hack is this year's spam signal.*

## Task playbooks

### 1. Content strategy + weekly calendar (2-person team)

**Quarterly setup:**
1. Pick exactly 3 content pillars. Each must map to a revenue line in BUSINESS-CONTEXT.md — {{FILL: e.g., SaaS problem-space, client-work lessons, e-com/music behind-the-scenes}}.
2. Write a one-line ICP per pillar: role + problem + what they already tried. Pin it above the idea bank.
3. Fill the idea bank to ≥15 ideas per pillar. Sources: support tickets, client questions, sales objections, our own metrics, things we fixed this week.
4. Score each idea 0–2 on: (a) first-hand proof exists, (b) evergreen ≥12 months, (c) maps to a CTA we care about. Only draft ideas scoring ≥4/6.
5. Idea bank entry format — one line per idea, so any of us (or the agent) can pick one up cold:
   ```
   [pillar tag] draft hook (one sentence) | proof source (metric/ticket/story) | CTA target | score /6
   ```
   An idea without a proof source is a title, not an idea. Don't bank it until you know what evidence backs it.

**Weekly calendar (pillar → repurpose model):**

| Day | Person A (maker) | Person B (editor/distributor) |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Pick topic from idea bank; research + outline pillar (2h) | Review outline, approve or redirect (15 min) |
| Tue | Draft pillar piece, no editing while drafting (2h) | Engagement window: reply to every comment/DM (30 min) |
| Wed | Self-edit pass (playbook 6) → publish pillar (1h) | Cut LinkedIn post + X thread from pillar (1h) |
| Thu | Newsletter issue (playbook 3) (1.5h) | IG asset + short-video script from pillar (1h) |
| Fri | Joint 30-min metrics review: replies/saves/signups, not likes | Refill idea bank (+3 minimum); schedule next week's repurposes (1h) |

**Rules:**
- Swap A/B roles weekly. *Why: editing someone else's work is the fastest way to learn the craft.*
- Hard cap: pillar piece ≤4h door-to-door. Over cap = scope too big → cut to one idea, bank the rest.
- Idea bank <10 ideas → Friday becomes an idea sprint. Never pick topics on the day.
- Short week? Drop a repurpose. Never drop the pillar or the newsletter.

### 2. Long-form article production (pillar piece)

1. **Thesis first.** Write the takeaway in ≤20 words at the top of the doc. Can't → split into two articles.
2. **Research (45 min, time-boxed).** Collect 3–5 first-hand proof points (our numbers, client stories, screenshots) + max 2 external sources. Rule: only cite a link you actually opened.
3. **Outline before prose.** Hook → promise → 3–5 H2s (each H2 = one step or one claim, each with ≥1 concrete example slot) → CTA. Get the outline approved by the other person before drafting. *Why: a wrong outline costs 10 minutes to fix; a wrong draft costs 2 hours.*
4. **Draft ugly and fast.** No editing mid-draft. Target 1.3× final length — you will cut it back.
5. **Length band:** 1,000–1,600 words for evergreen. Under 800 → it's a social post, not an article. Over 2,000 → split.
6. **Headline sprint.** Write 10 options, then pick with this filter (all four required):
   - Names the reader's problem or a surprising outcome — not the topic category.
   - Contains a number, a named thing, or a timeframe.
   - ≤70 characters.
   - The body fully pays it off — no promise the piece doesn't keep.
7. **Format:** subhead every 150–300 words; no paragraph >4 lines on mobile; one bolded key line per section maximum.
8. **Run playbook 6 (self-edit), then RUBRIC.md.** Both pass → publish. Pillar pieces always get a `cjudge` review (see escalation).
9. **Same day:** extract the quote bank for repurposing (playbook 5, step 2).

### 3. Newsletter issue production

1. **One core idea per issue.** Test: if the reader only sees the first 3 lines, do they still get value? No → restructure.
2. **Subject lines: write 5, keep 1.** ≤45 characters (mobile truncation — verify current specifics). Must be true to the body — clickbait debt compounds in email.
3. **Preview text: write it deliberately** (40–90 characters). Never let the client default to "View in browser" or the first line. It's your second subject line.
4. **Structure (400–800 words):**
   - Personal open: 2–3 sentences, a real event from this week. Not "hope you're well."
   - Core idea: the claim, stated plainly.
   - Proof: one worked example, our numbers, or a short story.
   - One CTA in the body. One.
   - PS line.
5. **PS strategy:** the PS is the second-most-read line after the subject. Use it for the secondary ask: a reply prompt, a soft product mention, or next issue's teaser. Reply prompts win — replies build deliverability and tell us what resonates.
6. **Pre-send checks (binary):** test-send to self; click every link; read on a phone; personalization fallback set; unsubscribe link present.
7. **Measure:** replies and click-throughs per issue, not open rate (opens are inflated by privacy proxies — verify current specifics).

### 4. Platform-native social posts

Same idea, four different artifacts. Rewrite the hook for each platform — never paste across.

| Platform | Format | Hook convention | Length | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | 1–2 line paragraphs, heavy white space, no markdown | First ~200 characters show before "…more" — hook must work truncated (verify current specifics) | 150–300 words | Story → lesson → question. External links in first comment, not body (reach claim — verify current specifics). 0–3 niche hashtags. |
| X | Single post or numbered thread (5–9 tweets) | Tweet 1 = payoff + promise. Never "a thread on X 🧵" throat-clearing | ≤280 chars/tweet | One idea per tweet; each tweet must survive being screenshotted alone; last tweet = recap + CTA. |
| Instagram | Carousel or Reel carries the idea; caption supports | Caption line 1 lands before the ~125-char feed truncation (verify current specifics) | Slide text ≤10 words; caption 100–200 words | Slide 1 = hook only. One idea per slide. Final slide CTA = "save this." 3–5 niche hashtags (verify current guidance). |
| TikTok | Vertical 9:16, talking head or screen capture, captions on | Hook in first 1–2 seconds, spoken AND on-screen | 21–45s for tip content | Lo-fi native beats polished. Completion rate is the metric (verify current specifics). Payoff early, don't save it for the end. |

**Per-platform steps:**

LinkedIn:
1. Hook in 1–2 lines that still parses when cut at ~200 characters.
2. Body arc: situation (2–3 lines) → turn → lesson → receipts (numbers) → question.
3. Blank line every 1–2 sentences. No markdown symbols — LinkedIn renders `**` and `#` literally.
4. End on a question you genuinely want answered; reply to every comment in the first hour (early comments drive reach — verify current specifics).
5. Any external link goes in the first comment.

X:
1. Choose format: one claim → single post; framework/argument → thread.
2. Tweet 1 = stat or claim + result + explicit promise ("The exact sequence:").
3. Number the tweets ("1/"); one idea each; every tweet must parse alone as a screenshot.
4. Line breaks inside tweets for rhythm; 0–1 hashtags.
5. Final tweet: recap + the only link in the thread.

Instagram:
1. Pick the carrier: carousel for frameworks/lists, Reel for story/demo.
2. Slide 1 = hook only, ≤10 words. One idea per slide. Last slide = "save this" CTA.
3. Caption: hook before the ~125-char fold; body adds context, never repeats the slides; 3–5 niche hashtags (verify current guidance).
4. Build from the saved slide template — design once per quarter, swap text weekly.

TikTok / Reels / Shorts:
1. Script before filming: spoken hook ≤2s, core payoff by the 1/3 mark, CTA in the last 5s.
2. Write the on-screen hook text separately from the spoken hook — they should differ, not duplicate.
3. Captions always on; vertical 9:16; audio clarity matters more than image quality.
4. Lo-fi single take beats produced for tip content; batch-film 3–4 scripts per session.
5. CTA = comment keyword ("comment EMAILS") or follow — never a URL read aloud.

**Every post, before scheduling:** one CTA, temperature-matched (principle 9 — social is cold: ask for follow/save/reply, not purchase). Then run RUBRIC gates G1–G7.

### 5. Repurposing pipeline (1 pillar → 5+ assets)

**Conversion table:**

| Pillar element | Becomes | Platform | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thesis + strongest stat | Story post with rewritten hook | LinkedIn | 20 min |
| Full argument | Numbered thread (5–9 tweets) | X | 30 min |
| Framework / numbered steps | Carousel, one step per slide | Instagram (+ LinkedIn doc post) | 45 min |
| Sharpest single claim | Standalone contrarian post | X | 5 min |
| Before/after example | 30–45s talking-head script | TikTok / Reels / Shorts | 30 min |
| Whole piece | Newsletter feature + link | Email | 20 min |
| Best one-liner | Quote graphic | IG story / X | 10 min |

**Steps:**
1. Publish the pillar (Wednesday in the standard calendar).
2. Same day, build the quote bank: copy every standalone line, stat, and step into a scratch file (10 min). This is the raw material for everything below.
3. Cut a minimum of 5 assets using the table. Rewrite every hook natively — copy-paste fails RUBRIC gate G6.
4. Spread publishing over the following 7–10 days. Never dump all assets on one day.
5. Track each asset back to its pillar (same slug in the filename/tracker) so Friday metrics roll up per pillar topic, not per post.
6. After 6–8 weeks: re-run anything that landed in our top 20% engagement, with a fresh hook. *Why: most followers never saw it the first time.*
7. See `examples/repurposing-set.md` for one pillar fully converted.

### 6. The self-edit pass (mechanical, in order)

1. **Gap rule:** minimum 2h between finishing the draft and editing it. *Why: fresh, you read what you meant, not what you wrote.*
2. **Hook gate first.** Line 1 contains ≥1 of: a number, a named pain, a question, a contrarian claim. Fails → fix before anything else.
3. **One-idea test.** Takeaway in ≤20 words at the top. Delete or bank every paragraph that doesn't serve it.
4. **First-100-words test.** The reader knows exactly what they'll get by word 100.
5. **Cut ratio: delete ≥15% of words.** Count before and after. Under 10% cut = you skimmed; go again.
6. **Sentence pass.** Split every sentence >25 words. Break every paragraph >4 lines (check on mobile width).
7. **Readability score:** Hemingway grade ≤8 for social and newsletter, ≤10 for long-form. Over → shorten sentences, swap latinate words for plain ones.
8. **Specificity sweep.** Search for: very, significantly, many, a lot, often, recently, improved, huge. Each hit → replace with a number, date, or name — or delete the claim.
9. **Kill-list grep.** Search every phrase in RUBRIC.md kill list. Zero matches allowed.
10. **Claims audit.** Every stat/claim is (a) our data, (b) a link you opened, or (c) explicitly framed as opinion. Orphan claims get cut or sourced.
11. **Read aloud once at full speed.** Anywhere you stumble, rewrite that line.
12. **CTA check.** Exactly one primary CTA; temperature-matched (principle 9).
13. **Score against RUBRIC.md.** All gates pass and score ≥ threshold → publish. Any dimension ≤2 after one revision → escalate.

### 7. Files, naming, and workflow states

1. All drafts live in {{FILL: content working directory, e.g. ~/work/content/}}, one file per asset.
2. Filename: `YYYY-MM-DD--pillar-slug--platform.md` (e.g. `2026-07-09--onboarding-emails--x-thread.md`). The shared slug is what lets Friday metrics roll up per pillar.
3. Every asset moves through these states, in order, tracked in the file's header line:
   `idea → outlined → drafted → edited → scored → scheduled → published → repurposed`
4. Hard rule: nothing skips `edited` (playbook 6) or `scored` (RUBRIC.md). An agent that drafts a piece stops at `drafted` and hands off — a human or `cjudge` moves it past `scored`.
5. Tracker row per asset: date · pillar · platform · CTA target · rubric score · day-7 outcome metric (replies/saves/signups — not likes).
6. Kill the guilt pile: any idea still at `idea` after 8 weeks gets deleted or re-scored. A stale bank hides the good ideas.

## Junior mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---|---|
| Throat-clearing intro ("In this article we'll explore…") | Delete paragraph 1. The real hook is usually hiding in paragraph 2. |
| Writing to impress peers instead of helping the ICP | Use jargon only if the ICP in BUSINESS-CONTEXT.md uses it. You're paid in "this helped," not "this sounded smart." |
| Describing features instead of outcomes | For every "it does X," add "so you get Y" — then usually delete the "it does X." |
| Three CTAs in one piece | One. The others become their own posts. |
| Publishing the first draft | Editing is the job. If you cut <15%, you didn't edit (playbook 6, step 5). |
| Pasting the same text to 4 platforms | Rewrite the hook per platform (gate G6). Ten extra minutes, multiple times the reach. |
| Chasing a trend outside our pillars | If it doesn't map to a pillar, skip it — reach without ICP fit is vanity traffic. |
| Judging by likes | Count replies, saves, profile clicks, email signups, DMs, sales conversations. Likes are applause; replies are pipeline. |
| "Studies show…" with no link | Our data, an opened link, or honest opinion framing. Nothing else ships. |
| Hook promises X, body delivers Y | The reader remembers the debt, not the post. True hooks only — sharpen the body instead. |
| Waiting for inspiration | The idea bank and the calendar are the muse. Topic selection happens Friday, never day-of. |
| Three days polishing one post | 4h cap on pillars, 1h on social. Ship at 7/10 (principle 8) — the next rep teaches more than the next polish. |

## Escalate to frontier review when…

High-stakes = wrong version costs money, trust, or a relationship — not just engagement.
Run `cjudge <file>` (second review by a frontier model) **before publishing** if ANY of these is true:

1. The piece makes a claim about a named person, company, or competitor.
2. Client work is mentioned — even praise — and written permission isn't on file.
3. It contains revenue figures, income claims, guarantees, pricing commitments, or legal/health/finance-adjacent advice.
4. A statistic can't be sourced in one click.
5. It goes to the full email list or anything unretractable (launch post, paid placement, partner co-post).
6. It's a pillar piece — errors multiply into 5+ downstream assets.
7. It's a contrarian/spicy take. Apply the screenshot test: still fine when screenshotted out of context by a hostile reader?
8. It's the first piece in a new format or platform — it becomes the template everything else copies.
9. RUBRIC result is borderline: any gate ambiguous, or any dimension still ≤2 after one revision.
10. It's reactive comms (responding to criticism, an outage, drama). Always, no exceptions.

**What the cjudge review must check:** factual claims against sources, tone against STYLE-VOICE.md, the ten triggers above, and RUBRIC gates. Log the verdict next to the piece in the tracker.
