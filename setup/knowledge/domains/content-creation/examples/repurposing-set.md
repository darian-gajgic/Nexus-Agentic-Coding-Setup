## Asset 1 — LinkedIn post (story format, 150–300 words)

We lost 358 of our last 412 trial signups in one day.

Not to a competitor. To silence — they logged in once and never came back.

I blamed the product for months. Then I actually read our onboarding emails like a stranger would:

→ Email 1: welcome + founder story
→ Email 2: feature tour
→ Email 3: case study
→ Email 4: webinar invite
→ Email 5: discount

Every single one was selling the product.

To people who had already signed up.

That sale is closed. The sale we kept losing was much smaller: "will you spend 10 more minutes and generate your first report?"

Users who generated one report in week 1 converted at 41%.
Users who didn't: 3%.

So we rewrote the sequence around one rule:

**One email. One action. One button.**

Day 0: deliver a win, not a welcome
Day 1: remove the setup blocker (honestly — "this takes 4 minutes and it's the least fun part")
Day 3: show users their own data
Day 5: name the objection out loud
Day 7: offer the exit ("reply and I'll tell you straight if we're the wrong tool")

Eight weeks later: trial-to-paid up from 9% to 14%. No new features.

The email that scared me most — day 7, offering the exit — became the one that closes deals a discount never touched.

What's the one action in YOUR product that separates converts from ghosts?

**3/** The data that changed everything:

Trial users who generated 1 report in week one → 41% converted.
Users who didn't → 3%.

Your product has this number too. Find it before writing a single email.

**4/** The rule for every email after that:

One email. One action. One button.

Cover the body. If subject line + button text alone don't tell you the single action wanted, rewrite it.

**5/** Day 0 — deliver a win, not a welcome.

Subject: "Your first report is 3 clicks away"

No founder story. No feature tour. The fastest possible path to visible output.

**6/** Day 1 — remove the setup blocker. Honestly.

"Connecting your data takes 4 minutes and it's the least fun part of the product."

Naming the friction took our click-through from 6% → 19%. Pretending setup is "instant" fools nobody.

**7/** Day 3 — show users their own data.

"You've generated 2 reports. That's ~2 hours you didn't spend formatting spreadsheets."

Inactive user? Branch the email. Progress stats sent to someone who did nothing read as sarcasm.

**8/** Day 5 — say the quiet part.

"Most trials stall here because the reports feel auto-generated. Here's the white-label setting."

Your day-5 email = your #1 week-one support question, answered before it's asked.

**9/** Day 7 — the honest close.

"If it saved you time, here's the annual plan. If not, reply and I'll tell you straight if we're the wrong tool."

Offering the exit closed more deals than the discount ever did.

## Why this works

- **One idea, four hooks — this is the whole lesson.** Same pillar, but LinkedIn opens on the *story* ("we lost 358…"), X opens on the *stat + promise*, IG opens on the *stat alone in 5 words*, video opens on *stat + reassurance twist*. Principle 7 and gate G6: the idea travels, the packaging never does.
- **Hook comparison at a glance:**

| Platform | Hook | Why this angle |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | "We lost 358 of our last 412 trial signups in one day." | Feed rewards first-person business narrative; concrete count beats percentage for story-feel. |
| X | "87% of our trial signups logged in once and never came back." + result + "The exact sequence:" | Scanners need stat, payoff, and an explicit promise inside tweet 1. |
| Instagram | "87% of trial users never come back." | Must survive as 5 words on a slide and ~125 caption chars. |
| TikTok | Same stat + "…it's probably not your product." | Video needs a curiosity twist in 2 seconds, or the swipe wins. |

- **CTAs step down the temperature ladder (principle 9):** LinkedIn asks a discussion question (comments = reach), X links the pillar (warmest social readers), IG asks for a save (platform-native currency), video asks for a comment keyword (algorithmic reply loop). Nobody is asked to buy — cold channels feed the newsletter, the newsletter sells.
- **Each asset is self-sufficient.** No "as I wrote in my article…" — a reader who never sees the pillar still gets a complete idea (principle 2). The pillar link is a bonus, not a dependency.
- **The thread's tweets are screenshot-safe by design:** each one restates enough context to parse alone, because threads travel as screenshots of single tweets more than as threads.
- **Format effort matches the table in playbook 5:** ~20 min LinkedIn, ~30 min thread, ~45 min carousel, ~30 min script = the "5+ assets from one pillar" economics that make a 2-person calendar sustainable (principle 6).
- **Platform mechanics are tagged, not asserted** — link-in-comment, truncation lengths, completion-rate weighting all carry "(verify current specifics)" per principle 10, so the exemplar won't silently rot as platforms change.
