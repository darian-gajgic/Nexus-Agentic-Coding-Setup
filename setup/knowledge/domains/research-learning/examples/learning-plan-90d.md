# 90-day learning plan: Marketing — junior A.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Skill** | Marketing for our own products (positioning, copy, email, paid, SEO basics) |
| **Person / quarter** | A. · 2026-07-06 → 2026-10-02 (13 weeks) |
| **Time budget** | 8 h/week of protected skill time (Mon brief 0.5 h · build ~6 h · Fri review 1.5 h) |
| **Practice ground** | InvoiceRadar (our SaaS) as client zero: live landing page (baseline 3.1% visitor→trial), email list of 420, 2 blog posts, zero paid history |

## Definition of "working professional" — the exit bar

By 2026-10-02, A. can, without AI drafting for them:

- **C1 Positioning** — produce a positioning one-pager (audience, alternatives,
  differentiator, proof) that the other founder can pitch from cold; scores ≥3 on the
  marketing rubric.
- **C2 Conversion copy** — rewrite a landing page and beat its 4-week baseline
  visitor→trial rate by ≥20% relative (3.1% → ≥3.7%), measured, not vibes.
- **C3 Email** — design, write, and send a 3-email sequence to the real list hitting
  ≥35% open and ≥1.5% click, with a results memo.
- **C4 Paid test** — design, run, and read a €150 ad test with kill thresholds set
  before launch, ending in a kill/scale memo a stranger could act on.
- **C5 SEO/content** — write a search-targeted brief + publish the page + define the
  measurement plan. (Rankings lag past 90 days; the tested capability is
  brief→page→tracking, not the ranking itself.)

Targets are learning thermostats, not performance reviews: a miss triggers a Friday
diagnosis, never a rewrite of the bar mid-quarter.

## Day-1 baseline (before any studying)

90 minutes, timed, no AI: write (a) a positioning one-pager and (b) a landing-page hero
section for InvoiceRadar. Score against the marketing rubric; file the artifact and the
score in `~/knowledge/feedback/a-lessons.md`. This is the before-photo — week 13 repeats
the exact task.

## The weekly engine (fixed, all 13 weeks)

1. **Mon, 30 min — brief**: name the deliverable, the one technique being practiced,
   and a predicted rubric score.
2. **Tue–Thu, ~6 h — build** with AI in its phase role (below).
3. **Fri, 90 min — review**: run the review-and-learn loop (Playbook 5): predict →
   compare → classify → score vs rubric → log 1–3 lessons → 2-minute teach-back to the
   other founder → answer the 5 weekly-review questions.

AI-role ratchet (Playbook 4.6) — advance only after 2 consecutive deliverables ≥3 avg:

- **Phase 1 (W1–4)**: A. directs and reviews, AI drafts. Learning by reviewing the best —
  full predict-then-compare on every draft.
- **Phase 2 (W5–8)**: A. drafts, AI critiques against the rubric.
- **Phase 3 (W9–12)**: A. works solo; AI reviews only the finished artifact
  (plus one pre-launch safety check on anything spending money or emailing the list).

## Week-by-week

**W1 — Measure before touching anything**
- Ship: tracking audit + baseline memo (traffic, 3.1% trial rate, list size, open/click
  history, current rankings). Plus the Day-1 baseline self-test.
- AI role: generates the audit checklist; A. verifies every number in the real dashboards.
- Check: every C1–C5 target has a written baseline number. No baseline = C2's "+20%" is
  unprovable later.

**W2 — Voice of customer**
- Ship: VoC doc — 2 live prospect/user interviews + 30 mined datapoints (competitor
  reviews, our support emails), verbatims tagged pain/gain/objection.
- AI role: drafts interview guide, tags transcripts; A. conducts both interviews live.
- Check: ≥15 usable verbatims; every tag traceable to a quote.

**W3 — Positioning one-pager (C1, attempt 1)**
- Ship: positioning doc built strictly from W2 verbatims. AI drafts 2 variants; A. merges,
  edits, and can defend every line.
- Check: the other founder pitches InvoiceRadar from the doc, cold, in 2 minutes. Stumbles
  = the doc's fault, revise.

**W4 — Landing page rewrite goes live (C2 starts) · SELF-TEST 1**
- Ship: rewritten page (hero, proof, objections section) live; measurement running.
- Self-test: 60 min, no AI — hero + 3 headline variants for a product the other founder
  picks at random. Score vs rubric.
- Check: page live by Thu; predicted vs actual rubric score gap discussed Friday.

**W5 — Welcome sequence (C3 starts)** — Phase 2 begins if the ratchet condition is met
- Ship: 3-email welcome automation live for new trials. A. drafts, AI critiques twice.
- Check: sequence live; each email has one job and one CTA (rubric line).

**W6 — Campaign to the real list**
- Ship: one campaign (feature/offer announcement) to all 420 subs + results memo vs
  open/click targets within 72 h.
- Check: results memo separates facts (numbers) from read (why), per research playbook 3.

**W7 — SEO brief + page 1 (C5)**
- Ship: search-targeted brief (keyword, intent, outline, internal links) → page published.
- AI role: critiques the brief BEFORE writing starts — brief quality gates page quality.
- Check: brief scores ≥3 before drafting begins; page indexed within the week.

**W8 — Paid test design + launch (C4 starts) · SELF-TEST 2**
- Ship: €75 test live — 2 audiences × 1 offer, kill thresholds written down BEFORE launch
  (e.g. kill any ad set >€25 spend with 0 trials).
- Self-test: 90 min, no AI — full campaign plan + first email for a goal the other
  founder sets. Score vs rubric; compare with W4 and baseline.
- Check: thresholds pre-committed in the memo; AI pre-launch safety check done.

**W9 — Paid test read + iteration (C4 completes)** — Phase 3 begins if ratchet met
- Ship: kill/scale memo on round 1 (cost per trial vs LTV math) + second €75 round
  applying the read.
- Check: memo's recommendation strength matches its evidence (research rubric G4 applies
  to marketing memos too).

**W10 — SEO page 2 + measurement plan (C5 completes)**
- Ship: second page + internal linking + a tracking plan naming who checks what monthly.
- AI role: end-review only.
- Check: solo rubric score ≥3 without AI drafting = C5 bar met.

**W11 — Integration: mini-launch, solo**
- Ship: one small end-to-end campaign — {{FILL: pick a real occasion — annual-plan promo,
  feature launch, seasonal offer}} — landing variant + 2 emails + 1 paid boost, planned
  and executed by A. alone. AI: one pre-launch safety check, one post-hoc review.
- Check: every C1–C4 muscle used once; launch memo written before, results memo after.

**W12 — Read results + harvest**
- Ship: mini-launch analysis + one optimization shipped from it + lessons harvest:
  promote the quarter's top 3 recurring lessons into the marketing PLAYBOOK/RUBRIC
  (Playbook 5.7 — this is how the Business Brain compounds).
- Check: ≥1 concrete rubric/playbook line added, dated, initialed.

**W13 — Exit test + retro**
- Self-test 3: repeat the Day-1 task exactly (90 min, no AI). Compare scores.
- Quarter retro (with the other founder): exit bar C1–C5 — met/not, evidence linked;
  decide next quarter (deepen marketing vs new skill) from the scores, not vibes.

## Spaced review (10–15 min, baked into existing slots)

- Every Mon brief starts by skimming last week's lessons log entries.
- First Friday each month: cold-redo one 4-week-old lesson situation (pick the one you'd
  least like to be tested on). Fail → it returns next month.
- Self-tests W4/W8/W13 re-test old material by design — that IS the spaced repetition.

## Kill / rebuild criteria (binary — Playbook 4.8)

- 2 consecutive weeks with nothing shipped → stop, diagnose (too big? fake project?
  missing prerequisite?), rebuild remaining weeks. Do not push through.
- Self-test score flat from W4 to W8 (<+0.5 avg) → same: rebuild, don't grind.
- Business emergency eats a week → shrink that week's deliverable, never skip the Friday
  review; two shrunken weeks in a row counts as "nothing shipped".

## Why this works

- **Measurement before movement (W1)** — juniors want to start by making things; pros
  start by making the *scoreboard*. Without W1, capability C2 is unfalsifiable and the
  whole quarter degrades into vibes (Rubric G9's "standard Y" needs a baseline).
- **VoC before positioning before copy (W2→W3→W4)** — copy is assembled from customers'
  words, not invented. This ordering is the single most professional habit in the plan
  and the least googleable: amateurs write, pros quote.
- **Every week ships to reality** — the list is real, the page is live, the €150 is real
  money. Feedback you can't argue with (Principle 10; Gate G10). Client-zero projects
  count because consequences, not clients, are what make practice deliberate.
- **The AI ratchet runs review→draft→solo** — in phase 1 the junior can't yet generate
  professional work but CAN diff their guess against strong drafts (Principle 9); by
  phase 3 the AI is a reviewer, which is the working relationship we actually want
  long-term.
- **Kill thresholds before the paid launch (W8)** — pre-commitment beats motivated
  reasoning; deciding what "failed" means after seeing the data is the amateur tell the
  research rubric calls overreach (G4), applied to marketing.
- **Self-tests are solo and timed** — AI-assisted scores measure the team; solo scores
  measure the human. Only the second one is "learning" (Gate G11).
- **W12 harvest writes rules, not memories** — lessons that don't graduate into a
  checklist are lost by next quarter (Playbook 5.7).
- **Kill criteria are stated up front** — a plan you can't fail is theater; a plan that
  fails loudly gets rebuilt in week 5 instead of mourned in week 13 (Gate G12).
