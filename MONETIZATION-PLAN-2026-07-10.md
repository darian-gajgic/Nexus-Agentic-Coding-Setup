## PART 2 — HOW to make money WITH the tool (step-by-step)

This is your consulting-bizdev playbook applied. The tool is the delivery engine; these steps are the business.

**Phase A — Package (week 1, ~4 h).**
1. Pick ONE offer from the shortlist. Recommended first: *"Automation audit + first workflow build, fixed price, 5 working days."* One problem you can demonstrably solve, one buyer type.
2. Name the buyer precisely (playbook rule): role + company type + size band you can list 50 of in an afternoon (e.g. "operations lead at a 5–30-person DACH e-commerce shop"). Narrow until it hurts.
3. Write the offer as one outcome sentence with a number and a fixed price (one number, not a range). Add an included list AND a ≥3-item excluded list (the exclusions defend the price). Add one risk-reversal (paid pilot / kill-switch after step 1).
4. Build the showable artifact template in the tool NOW (a sample audit for a fictional but realistic shop) — your proof-of-work substitute for a portfolio.

**Phase B — Prospect (weekly, ongoing, ~3–4 h/wk).**
5. Build a list of 25 symptom-matched prospects (observable pain: slow site, manual process visible in job posts, dormant automation). Symptom-first, never firmographic-only.
6. Per prospect, 3-minute homework → ONE specific verifiable fact (their LCP score, the manual process they advertise hiring for). That fact IS your personalization.
7. Send the 3-touch sequence (day 0 / 3–4 / 8–10), Tue–Thu 08:00–10:30 their time, then stop. Reply to everything within 4 business hours.
8. **Use the tool to scale the homework, not to spam:** run each prospect's site/data through a mini-audit so every email carries a real, specific finding. This is the tool's highest-leverage business use — it makes 25 deeply-personalized touches take an hour.

**Phase C — Close (per reply).**
9. Discovery call (30 min): make them quantify the pain ("what does this cost per month?"), bracket the price out loud on the call, schedule the next step ON the call. Deposit starts the clock — no deposit, no work.
10. Deliver the audit fast (tool does the heavy lifting; you review, verify, add judgment). Ship visible value within 5 working days — speed is your unfair advantage over agencies.

**Phase D — Compound (after each job).**
11. Negotiate case-study rights at signing; capture before/after baselines at kickoff. Every job → one showable artifact → the next sale is easier.
12. Convert one-off clients to retainers (A2) at delivery: "I can keep this running / extend it for €X/mo." Recurring revenue is the goal; acquisition is the cost.
13. Feed wins/losses into `~/knowledge/feedback/` so the tool's quality on YOUR niche compounds (this is what the learning loops are for — your delivery gets better and cheaper every month).

**Realistic trajectory (consistent weekly effort, no audience):** first paid gig in weeks-to-2-months; €2–8k/month within 6–12 months as retainers stack. This is labor income with a margin multiplier, not passive — but it's the path the data actually supports, and it doubles as the customer research that would de-risk any future product.

## PART 3 — Top 3 ways to make money FROM the tool itself

Honest first: **all three have median outcomes near €0** and monetize an *audience you don't have yet*. They are power-law lotteries; do them as marketing for Part 2, not as income. Ranked best-first for a junior.

### Strategy 1 — Open-source core + "build in public" (highest success as a FUNNEL, not as donations)
- **What:** open-source a cleaned, genericized slice (the grounded-critic loop or the eval harness — NOT your personal knowledge base, secrets, or the ToS-violating subscription plumbing), and document the build publicly (X/LinkedIn/dev.to, a demo video).
- **Why it ranks #1:** OSS donations median ≈ €0 (fewer than 12% of OSS devs earn anything), but the *credibility and inbound* it generates directly feeds Part 2 — you become "the person who built the AI verification system," which sells audits and automation builds. Conversion of OSS→paid hosting is 1–5% at scale you lack; conversion of OSS-reputation→consulting leads is far higher for a junior.
- **Steps:** (1) extract a self-contained, secret-free module; (2) MIT-license it, write a real README with a 30-second demo GIF; (3) publish + a launch post telling the story (the reports-1-and-2 experiment is a genuinely good hook); (4) add a GitHub Sponsors button and a one-line "I build these for clients →" link to your offer; (5) post progress weekly. **Time to first value:** 2–4 weeks. **Revenue:** donations €0–50/mo (ignore); leads = the real return. **Margin:** n/a. **Success as a lead funnel: M; as direct income: L.**
- **Hard gate:** publish nothing that routes others' work through your Claude/Z.AI subscriptions or exposes secrets — that's the ToS line and the security risk from your own audit.

### Strategy 2 — Productized "install + implement" service on top of the OSS (best REVENUE per hour)
- **What:** sell setting the system up for other small agencies/freelancers who saw the OSS — "I'll deploy your own verification-loop agent system and train you, fixed price."
- **Why #2:** it's really Part 2 with the tool as the product's subject; day rates for AI-agent implementation are the strongest verified demand signal (AI-integration demand +178% YoY). Legal (client uses their own API keys), high-margin, and each install is a case study.
- **Steps:** (1) package a fixed "agent-system setup: your keys, your machine, 1 week, €X" offer; (2) target the OSS audience + small agencies; (3) deliver via the same Part 2 sales motion; (4) upsell a maintenance retainer. **Time to first value:** 4–8 weeks (needs the OSS/reputation from Strategy 1 first). **Revenue:** €1–5k per install + retainer. **Margin: H. Success: M** (gated by having an audience/proof).

### Strategy 3 — Teach it: content + a focused paid artifact (slowest, audience-gated)
- **What:** a narrow paid course/guide — NOT "AI agents" (saturated), but one specific outcome: *"Build a grounded verification loop that catches AI's confident mistakes."* Or a paid newsletter documenting real client results.
- **Why #3, lowest:** median course earns €0; 75% of Udemy instructors make <€1k/yr; paid newsletters need 5,000+ engaged subscribers first — the audience IS the job, and you're starting from zero. Only worth it once Strategy 1 has built a following.
- **Steps:** (1) publish free content for 3–6 months to build an email list (off Strategy 1's momentum); (2) when ~1k engaged subscribers exist, launch ONE $50–150 focused product priced >$100 for the higher average; (3) email-first launch. **Time to first value:** 6–12 months. **Revenue:** <€1k/yr first attempt, realistically. **Margin: H (digital). Success: L** (audience-gated).

### The three, ranked
| | Strategy | Best for | Time to $ | Margin | Success |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | OSS + build-in-public | **lead generation** | 2–4 wk | n/a | M (as funnel) |
| 2 | Install/implement service | **revenue** | 4–8 wk | H | M |
| 3 | Course/content | later, audience-gated | 6–12 mo | H | L |

**The synthesis:** Strategy 1 builds the reputation → Strategy 2 monetizes it as service revenue → Part 2's client work is the real business the whole time → Strategy 3 only much later. None of the "sell the tool" paths beat "sell what the tool makes."

## PART 4 — The honest bottom line
- **Do:** Part 2 (services), with A1/A2/B1 as the beachhead, tool as the margin engine. This is the only path with strong, verified near-term demand for a solo junior.
- **Do (as marketing):** Strategy 1 OSS build-in-public — for reputation and inbound, expecting €0 in donations.
- **Then:** Strategy 2 install service once you have proof; convert clients to retainers throughout.
- **Avoid / defer:** selling the tool as a product, courses, and any collapsed-commodity deliverable (bulk content, raw lists, boilerplates).
- **Prerequisite:** fill `BUSINESS-CONTEXT.md` (onboarding). Every number here sharpens with your real niche, capacity, and market.
- **The tool's true business value:** it lets a junior operation deliver senior-quality, verified work fast and cheap — that is a margin-and-speed multiplier on a services business, not a product in itself. Sell the outcomes; keep the engine.

**Learn:** the money follows trust and distribution, which the tool doesn't provide — so the winning move is to point the tool at *delivery* (where it's a genuine edge) and spend your scarce human hours on *outreach and proof* (where no tool can substitute for you). Price every offer against the client's alternative — hiring a senior or an agency — not against your near-zero token cost.
